04 · The safety gap

Earth screening tests miss what space allows

NASA screens spacecraft materials with an upward-burning test on Earth (NASA-STD-6001 Test 1), assumed to be the worst case. BASS-II found flames surviving in leaner air in microgravity.

1.5–1.9 points of O₂

Acrylic (PMMA) rods kept burning at lower oxygen on the ISS than on Earth. The cause is weak, slow airflow, a “Goldilocks” zone the report notes is typical of spacecraft ventilation near surfaces.

Report p. 50Report p. 76

Moon (0.16 g) and Mars (0.38 g)? PSI has no partial-gravity combustion data yet. The challenge brief notes that microgravity and Earth results could bound intermediate gravity, so these limits may fall between the two dots on each row until LUCI and FM2 measure them directly.

07 · Back on Earth

The same research protects people at home

Fire models

Microgravity flames are simpler, with no buoyant plume, so they are used to test the computer models applied to wildfires, building fires and waste-to-energy burners (BASS-II research impacts).

Extinguishers

Fine-water-mist extinguishers developed for the ISS now have practical uses on Earth.

Detection and gear

NASA work has improved smoke detection and firefighter equipment, including protective clothing and air tanks.
